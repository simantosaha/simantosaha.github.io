Research

My research focuses on applying advanced signal processing and AI techniques to develop solutions for biomedical applications, including brain-computer interfaces and the diagnosis and prognosis of various clinical conditions. One of the key translational challenges in this area is the variability of biomedical signals due to physiological, hardware, and environmental factors. The variability hinders the generalisability of an algorithm over time and across users. My research investigates the sources of variability, explores potential markers that describe variability, and develops reproducible pattern recognition solutions.

Brain-Computer Interfaces

My research investigates brain-computer interfaces (BCIs), with a particular focus on understanding and addressing variability in EEG-based sensorimotor BCIs. I explore signal processing, feature extraction, cortical source localisation, and machine-learning approaches to improve the robustness and generalisability of BCI systems.

A major focus is understanding inter-subject and intra-subject variability and developing methods that can support more reliable BCI performance across users and over time.

Biomedical Signal Processing

I develop signal-processing and computational methods for analysing complex biomedical signals. The research includes EEG, ECG, electrograms, photoplethysmography, and other physiological recordings.

Particular emphasis is placed on identifying meaningful signal characteristics in the presence of noise, artefacts, physiological variability, and measurement-related uncertainty.

AI and Machine Learning for Healthcare

My research applies machine learning and artificial intelligence to biomedical and clinical data to develop reproducible pattern-recognition and prediction systems.

I am particularly interested in sequence learning, deep learning, data-driven modelling, and methods that can account for variability and uncertainty in physiological data.

Clinical and Translational Research

My research aims to translate computational and signal-processing methods into clinically relevant applications. This includes developing approaches for diagnosis, prognosis, patient monitoring, and personalised healthcare.

The broader goal is to bridge the gap between methodological developments and practical biomedical applications while maintaining reproducibility and clinical relevance.

Current Research

My current research explores the use of advanced artificial intelligence, signal processing, and data-driven methods for developing robust and personalised biomedical technologies. A particular emphasis is placed on understanding variability in physiological signals and translating this knowledge into reliable solutions for healthcare and assistive technologies.
